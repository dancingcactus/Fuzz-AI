What carries a mood

  • Eyelids in the reel windows. An upper lid that drops and tilts (sleepy, sad, grumpy, smug) and a lower lid that rises for a happy squint. The lid angle does the job brows would, since there's no room for brows under the tuner.
  • The reels. Where they look, how big they are (small with fright, big with eagerness), and how fast they turn: racing when he's happy, crawling when he's bored, stopped when he's off, turning opposite ways when he's dizzy. They also swap for hearts, stars, spirals and shut-eye arcs.
  • The door. Eight mouths drawn on it (smile, grin, flat, frown, smirk, wobble, "o" and gritted teeth), and it opens to gasp, laugh, sob or sing. Dizzy spills a loop of tape like a tongue.
  • The hardware. The antenna perks up or droops, the handle jumps like raised brows, the tuner needle seeks while he thinks, a red record light blinks while he works, and the bulb on the antenna lights up when he's eager.
